## Our approach

UX Agency Finders is an editorial guide, not a review platform or a paid directory. We organise agencies by the markets they've actually worked in, because the best partner for a payments app is rarely the best partner for a hospital system.

## The eight industries

Healthcare, Fintech, Ecommerce, Logistics, Real Estate, Education, Robotics, and AI & ML. Robotics and AI & ML are technology markets where the products and user tasks are distinctive enough to need their own lists.

## What gets an agency included

An agency is included in an industry when **a public source names a relevant project and describes the agency's UX or product contribution** in that industry.

These don't count on their own:

- Visual style, or generic "SaaS" or "enterprise" experience
- A client logo with no described scope
- A marketing website for a company in the industry
- A service page claiming industry expertise

Capabilities such as dashboard design, design systems or AI interfaces can support a profile, but they never add an agency to an industry list by themselves.

### Clearly labelled exceptions

Occasionally we include an agency on the strength of its stated services rather than a named client case. CorLab Tech in Logistics is an example. When we do, the entry carries a visible **service-level evidence** label everywhere it appears, so you know to ask for a comparable project.

## How positions are set

Each industry has its **own editorial order**. We consider:

- how relevant the named project and its users are to the industry,
- how clearly the agency's contribution is described,
- the research, workflow and delivery detail that matters in that market,
- the work stage (concept, prototype, pilot or shipped) and any source limits.

**Positions aren't scores.** We don't calculate ratings, import review stars or claim measured market leadership. The first-listed agency isn't automatically right for every brief. Each entry's scope notes explain who it suits.

## Where our evidence comes from

Our evidence comes from **agencies' own published case studies and portfolio pages**, each linked from the relevant placement. We treat outcome figures, adoption numbers and compliance claims in those pages as agency-reported, and we don't present them as independently verified.

We don't publish budgets, hourly rates or availability, because they change often and vary by project. If we can't find public evidence of a capability, it doesn't mean the agency lacks it. It means we can't confirm it yet.

## Keeping lists current

We check sources and links regularly and review every industry list and profile at least every six months, or sooner when something material changes. "Updated" dates change only after a real review.
